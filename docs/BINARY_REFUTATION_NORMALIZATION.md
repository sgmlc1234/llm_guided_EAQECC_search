# Binary refutation normalization

The original binary certificates use `scripts/refutations/sat_normal_form_q2.py`.
They remain unchanged. For `j=n-k-c>=1`, the first radical row has interleaved
X/Z bits. The builder adds:

- `not x_i` for every coordinate;
- `z_1` to exclude the zero row;
- `z_i or not z_(i+1)` for adjacent coordinates.

These clauses express a nonempty Z-prefix with unspecified weight in one CNF.
There is no separate SAT call for each prefix weight. Other radical rows remain
subject to independence and Gram constraints, but are not forced to be Z-only.
For `j=0`, the builder instead normalizes the first hyperbolic row and requires
a prefix of weight at least the target distance; this is a different branch.

## Coordinate-normalization lemma

Lemma 2 assumes `dim L = dim R + 2`, `dim R > 2`, and every non-radical vector
has weight at least `n-1`. Its proof works over any finite field, including
F2. Thus it also applies to the seven archived binary k=1 exclusions at
`(n,c) = (6,1),(7,3),(8,3),(9,5),(10,5),(11,7),(13,8)` and `d=n-1`.
It permits all radical X bits to be zero. The existing first-row prefix
restriction remains compatible: after making every radical row Z-only,
coordinate permutations can place the first row's nonzero entries first.
No additional local maps are needed in the binary case.

The original binary CNFs use the weaker, complete prefix normalization.
The qutrit certificate uses the stronger lemma. The binary UNSAT proofs
already cover the larger search space; applying the lemma is an optional
strengthening, not a repair of the original certificates. A smaller formula
or a faster solve must be measured, not inferred from adding unit clauses.
The original k=2 exclusions do not satisfy the lemma's k=1 hypothesis.

Repository history records the binary builder in commit c90ce4f (9 September
2026) and the qutrit normalization metadata in a323216 (15 September 2026).
These are preservation dates, not evidence of when the lemma was first
discovered or why it was not applied to the original binary runs.

## Completed checks

On 22 September 2026 all seven binary CNFs were regenerated byte-for-byte.
Truth-assignment enumeration confirmed exactly n admissible Z-strings at
length n, one for each nonempty prefix. The
[prefix audit](../artifacts/refutations/normalization_review/prefix_audit.json)
records the clauses and original CNF hashes.

The [parity audit](../artifacts/refutations/normalization_review/parity_audit.json)
checks Theorem 1's binary coordinates at n=5..24. The symplectic pairing is
`n mod 2`. At even n the four-dimensional L has zero restricted Gram form,
so this particular formula loses its logical hyperbolic pair. This does not
exclude other even-length constructions. The separate preserved construction
at n=6,8,10 passes the exact family checker at c=n-4. Matching independent
nonexistence certificates establish the minima at the six lengths quoted
in Section 4.3; these finite checks are not a universal parity theorem.

The [new all-radical-Z replay](../artifacts/refutations/binary_normalization/2026-09-22/README.md)
completed all seven exclusions with checked DRAT proofs. A feasible
[[6,1,5;2]]2 positive control remains SAT and its decoded matrix passes the
independent checker. These are new, separately preserved certificates,
not replacements for the original runs. The entire replay took about
33 seconds locally; no cross-run speedup is asserted.
